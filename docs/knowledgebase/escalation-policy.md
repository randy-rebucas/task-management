# Escalation Policy

This document defines the escalation process for issues, blockers, and incidents that cannot be resolved at the initial level.

---

## Overview
Escalation ensures that unresolved issues receive the attention they need from the right people at the right time. All team members are encouraged to escalate promptly rather than let issues stall progress.

---

## Escalation Levels

### Level 1 – Team Level
- **Who:** Individual contributor
- **When:** Issue cannot be resolved independently within a reasonable timeframe
- **Action:** Raise the issue with your team lead or direct supervisor
- **Expected Response Time:** Within **4 business hours**

---

### Level 2 – Department Level
- **Who:** Team lead or department manager
- **When:** Issue cannot be resolved at team level within 1 business day
- **Action:** Escalate to the department head with a clear summary and impact assessment
- **Expected Response Time:** Within **1 business day**

---

### Level 3 – Cross-Department / Management Level
- **Who:** Department head
- **When:** Issue requires cross-departmental input or has significant business impact
- **Action:** Escalate to senior management or relevant department leads via formal communication
- **Expected Response Time:** Within **2 business days**

---

### Level 4 – Executive Level
- **Who:** Senior management
- **When:** Issue is critical, affects multiple departments, or has legal/financial implications
- **Action:** Escalate to executive leadership with full incident report
- **Expected Response Time:** **Immediate / same day**

## How to Escalate
1. Document the issue clearly — include context, timeline, and impact.
2. Notify the appropriate contact using the system's escalation/notification feature or direct communication.
3. Update the associated task or ticket with the escalation status.
4. Follow up if no response is received within the expected timeframe.

---

## Escalation Do's and Don'ts

**Do:**
- Be clear, factual, and concise when escalating
- Include evidence (screenshots, logs, reports)
- Follow the defined escalation path

**Don't:**
- Skip levels without a valid reason
- Escalate without attempting resolution at the current level
- Use escalation as a first response to minor issues

## Communication Templates

### Level 1 Escalation (to Team Lead)
> **Subject:** Escalation – [Brief Issue Title]
> Hi [Name], I'm unable to resolve the following issue independently and need your support.
> **Issue:** [Description]
> **Impact:** [What is affected]
> **Steps Taken:** [What you've already tried]
> **Urgency:** [Low / Medium / High]

### Level 2+ Escalation (to Dept Head / Management)
> **Subject:** Escalation – [Brief Issue Title] – [Date]
> **Raised By:** [Name, Role]
> **Date Raised:** [Date]
> **Issue Summary:** [Clear description]
> **Business Impact:** [What is at risk]
> **Previous Escalation Steps:** [Level 1 outcome]
> **Proposed Resolution:** [Optional]
> **Evidence Attached:** [Yes/No]

---

## Post-Escalation Review

After an escalation is resolved:
1. Document the root cause and resolution in the related task or ticket.
2. Identify whether a process change could prevent recurrence.
3. Share learnings with the team in the next stand-up or review meeting.
4. Update relevant documentation or FAQs if needed.

---

## SLA Tracking

All escalated issues should be tracked with:
- Date and time raised
- Level of escalation
- Person responsible at each level
- Resolution date and time
- Root cause summary
